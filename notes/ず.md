The auxiliary ず is a archaic negation suffix still used in formal contexts and idiomatic phrases today. The basic meaning is identical to ない, but the conjugation is different since it merged with the even older ん negative and has a combination form with あり, the あり形.

| 活用  | 未然形 | 連用形 | 終止形 | 連体形 | 己然形 | 命令形 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ず   | な   | に・ず | ず   | ぬ   | ね   |     |
| あり形 | ざら  | ざり  |     | ざる  | ざれ  | ざれ  |
Note that the 連体形, the only form that can be used attributively, remains different from the 終止形, which is an exception in modern Japanese common for archaic verbs. 
As auxiliary, it only occurs together with other verbs and (rarely) adjectives, interestingly only attaching to the archaic 未然形 of the preceding verb which does not always coincide with the modern form.

|     | 一段  | 五段  | サ変  | カ変  | 形容詞   | 形容動詞  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ----- | ----- |
| 未然形 | 見   | 学ば  | せ   | こ   | 少なから  | 華麗なら  |
| ず   | 見ず  | 学ばず | せず  | こず  | 少なからず | 華麗ならず |
The ず then conjugates as above, while the あり形 can be used to attach further auxiliaries. The ぬ-form has somewhat decoupled from that origin in modern Japanese, being used in sentence-final position according to the DOIJG, while ず cannot occur sentence-final anymore and is only used attributively. The merged negative ん reemerges in modern Japanese as contraction of ぬ.

**Examples**
1. 我儘にならないように我々は自分のことのみを考えるわけには｛いかぬ (modern)・いかず (archaic)｝。
2. 知らぬことを知らぬと言うには勇気が要る。
3. 朝ご飯を食べずに家を出てしまいました。
